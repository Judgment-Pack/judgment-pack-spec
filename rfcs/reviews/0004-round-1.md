## Findings and verdict (verbatim)

**Verdict: mergeable after fixes.** Rejecting this exploratory RFC is permitted. The problems are overstatements in the rationale, inconsistent public wording, and a stranded cross-reference in RFC 0006.

Reviewed commit `c5d72e467fd4468f67e51d81423844f88846429f` against its parent and the supplied runtime snapshot identified as `d891b05`. Reviewer: OpenAI Codex, 2026-09-21.

1. **MAJOR — RFC 0006 still assigns an unresolved question to the now-rejected RFC.**

   **At issue:** RFC 0006, lines 25–27 (`rfcs/0006-evaluator-conformance.md:25`): “Graph interaction and the selection probe are not Core questions and stay with … RFC 0002 … and … RFC 0004.”

   **Source:** RFC 0006’s selection-probe note at lines 284–287 advises separating an applicability check from full evaluation. That technical advice can survive rejection. Its adoption record, however, assigns continuing responsibility for the question to RFC 0004. Rejection strands that assignment. The disposition’s statement that the note “is … unaffected” does not account for this other passage.

   **Smallest fix:** Add a dated clarification to RFC 0006’s adoption record: RFC 0004 has been rejected; the selection-probe note remains informative advice for product implementations, with no active specification proposal carrying it. This preserves the historical adoption decision and requires no new mechanism.

2. **MAJOR — The site description converts a decision against standardization into an answer about standardizability.**

   **At issue:** web/build.py, line 456 (`web/build.py:456`): “Rejected proposal: whether any of a pack-selecting planner is standardizable. The recorded answer is no — a planner is product.”

   **Source:** RFC 0004’s disposition, lines 40–41, explicitly leaves whether a selection query could be portable unanswered because nothing was built to test it. Declining to standardize the proposed interface does not establish that no part is standardizable.

   **Smallest fix:** Describe the decision: “Rejected proposal: the project declines to standardize planner selection, including the proposed selection query.”

3. **MAJOR — Rejection does not establish that applicability can no longer be mistaken for authorization.**

   **At issue:** RFC 0004, line 34 (`rfcs/0004-planner-interface.md:34`): “With no standardized selection surface there is nothing to misread.”

   **Source:** The retained proposal, lines 93–94, warns against inferring authorization from applicability. RFC 0005, lines 51–53, makes the corresponding warning about selection metadata. Core still defines applicability, and product selectors can still exist. Neither cited document establishes that declining an interface eliminates this confusion.

   **Smallest fix:** Replace the sentence with a bounded claim: declining avoids adding a standardized selection surface; whether standardization would increase authorization confusion remains untested. Keep the existing authorization boundary.

4. **MINOR — The runtime’s decision is described as stronger opposition than its cited scope establishes.**

   **At issue:** RFC 0004, line 24 (`rfcs/0004-planner-interface.md:24`): “The one implementation took the opposite position on purpose.”

   **Source:** Runtime ADR-0012, lines 115–122, deliberately leaves choosing a pack for a request to the application. ADR-0007 excludes planner selection from its evaluator. But RFC 0004’s candidate interface returns candidates and already leaves authorization and subsequent product behavior outside the interface. Refusing to choose the pack is not, by itself, an explicit rejection of every candidate-query interface.

   **Smallest fix:** Change the heading to “The reference runtime deliberately leaves selection with the application.” The quotations and narrower factual attributions beneath it can remain.

5. **MINOR — The negative evidence is expressed as unbounded fact, and the inference about returnable data is too broad.**

   **At issue:** RFC 0004, lines 18–23 (`rfcs/0004-planner-interface.md:18`): “Nobody has asked”; “No such pair exists”; “both drafts that nothing emits, so a selection query has nothing to return”; and “no outside comment on any RFC.”

   **Source:** Both RFCs are indeed Draft. RFC 0001 expressly says its two-implementation evidence bar remains unmet; that does not establish zero producers. I found no producer of either proposed format in the inspected repositories. However, the runtime does emit a different, explicitly non-normative pack inventory through `packs list` and `list_packs` (`internal/project/inventory.go:155–208`). Absence of those proposed formats does not mean there are no pack references a product query could return.

   GOVERNANCE.md, lines 49–55, supports the project’s **recorded account** that public review draws no comments. The checkout neither contradicts that account nor independently establishes the stronger historical universal “no outside comment on any RFC.” It contains no complete comment archive. Likewise, it cannot establish that no interested product pair exists anywhere.

   **Smallest fix:** Attribute and bound the claims to the evidence considered or the maintainer’s knowledge. Replace “so a selection query has nothing to return” with the narrower absence of implementation/interoperability evidence for the proposed query. Apply the same qualification to CHANGELOG.md’s “no pair of products has asked.”

6. **MINOR — The claimed review archive does not yet contain this review.**

   **At issue:** RFC 0004, lines 9–11 (`rfcs/0004-planner-interface.md:9`): “The review of this disposition, with a written disposition for every finding, is recorded on the pull request that makes it and under `rfcs/reviews/`.”

   **Source:** At `c5d72e4`, that directory exists but contains no RFC 0004 review or disposition. I cannot establish the PR-comment half from this checkout. This is understandable during round 1, but the completed-record assertion is premature.

   **Smallest fix:** Before merge, publish this review and its maintainer dispositions and link the actual record. Alternatively, remove the additional claim about an in-repository copy if none will be committed. GOVERNANCE requires the PR record; it does not independently require a `rfcs/reviews/` copy.

7. **MINOR — Some public descriptions still imply an exclusively open proposal collection or an active standardized-selection path.**

   **At issue:**

   - FAQ.md, lines 131–134 (`FAQ.md:131`): “A standard answer needs a discovery and selection contract,” followed by the RFC references.
   - README.md, line 155 (`README.md:155`): “Open change proposals (RFCs), including the process.”
   - web/build.py, line 412 (`web/build.py:412`): “Open Requests for Comments — design proposals that are not part of the specification.”
   - The same file, line 1980: “Open design proposals and the change process.”

   **Source:** The RFC index now includes a rejected selection proposal and retained disposition records. The directory descriptions were already incomplete for accepted RFCs; this commit adds another closed record. FAQ Q23’s conditional statement is not logically false, but it leaves readers with an apparent standardization path without telling them selection was explicitly declined.

   **Smallest fix:** Describe the collection as proposals **and disposition records**. Add one sentence to Q23 distinguishing the still-proposed discovery formats from selection, which RFC 0004 leaves to products.

The remaining requested checks support the change:

- **Proposal preservation:** Everything from `## Summary` through EOF is byte-identical to the parent: **2,608 bytes**, with identical SHA-256 hashes. Git history also confirms the original RFC named product-only as its likely outcome and explicitly contemplated `Rejected`.
- **Process:** RFC 0000 lists `Rejected` without restricting it to standards-track RFCs, requiring a prior `Review` status, or requiring author withdrawal instead. GOVERNANCE explicitly permits rejection during the preview and presently permits the single maintainer to disposition their own RFC. Two independent implementations are an acceptance bar for stable normative features, not a rejection prerequisite. No additional substantive rejection section is required here. The required PR review and per-finding dispositions remain merge conditions.
- **Runtime citations:** Both files exist. The ADR-0012 quotation is exact, allowing for line wrapping, and its attribution of the reason “applicability is not authorization” is fair. ADR-0007 explicitly excludes planner selection from the evaluator.
- **Runtime implementation search:** I found no context-based planner, candidate-pack selector, or applicability-ranking surface contradicting the limited claim that this proposal was not prototyped there. `list_packs` lists configured inventory; `selection(id)` selects caller-named IDs; `candidates.go` generates test inputs. Ranking matches concerned other functions.
- **Security and scope references:** RFC 0005 explicitly says selection metadata is a hint, never authorization. The non-goals explicitly exclude runtime orchestration from the core preview.
- **Other references:** FAQ Q7 already says the planner is product. The architecture diagram, updated table, RFC index, and annotated seeded-proposals list are consistent. The earlier changelog entry describes historical introduction and need not be rewritten. RFC 0005’s selection-interface question remains a legitimate unresolved question; its deferred restatement is disclosed, though marking its link “rejected” would improve clarity.

Merge after addressing findings 1–7 and recording the required maintainer dispositions. No new mechanism or change to the preserved proposal is needed.

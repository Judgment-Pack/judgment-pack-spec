## 2. New findings

### R3-1 — MAJOR: Reporting failure has no stated relationship to the original tool’s outcome

**Exact sentence, Evidence 3, line 124:**

> A reporter would be a `tool_post_invoke` hook.

**Design problem:** The hook placement is supported by the plugins note. What remains unspecified is what happens to the original call’s result when obtaining its reported receipt fails.

Clause 2 asks which upstream outcomes are reported. Clause 7 asks about account delivery, acknowledgements and retries. Neither explicitly asks whether the plugin waits for receipting before returning the tool result, queues reporting independently, or withholds an already-produced result when reporting fails.

This matters for a tool with side effects: reporting can fail **after the tool completed a write**. Returning an undifferentiated tool error can prompt the caller to repeat that write. Retrying submission of an existing account and rerunning the tool are different operations. A lost reporting acknowledgement also leaves receipt creation uncertain.

The existing MCP frontend expressly distinguishes a signer refusal from an unknown outcome and states its retry behavior; the proposed reporting path needs its own question about these distinctions. [MCP refusal contract](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/mcp-server.md#L161)

**Smallest edit:** Add to clause 7, with ownership assigned to the reporting plugin’s project and the gateway’s acknowledgement contract:

> Does reporting complete before the original tool result is returned, or proceed independently? If reporting is refused or its outcome is unknown, what result and reporting status reach the caller? Retrying the account must be distinguished from rerunning the tool: a reporting failure does not establish that the original call failed or that its effects were undone.

Leave the delivery policy and mechanism unchosen.

### R3-2 — MINOR: “Accepted” leaves the seal’s counting boundary ambiguous

**Exact sentence, clause 7, lines 261–262:**

> Whatever the answers, **a sealed count establishes the accounts the engine accepted, never that every routed call was reported.**

**Source/problem:** “Accepted” is defensible if it means successfully minted receipts, but the record does not define it that way.

The implementation distinguishes admission from minting:

- `admit` reserves in-flight work.
- `state.index` advances only after stamping succeeds.
- `release` handles failed work without incrementing that count.
- `sealSession` signs `state.index` after in-flight work finishes.

Consequently, a successful seal can follow admitted requests that produced no receipts. The seal itself authenticates a declared count; the verified store supplies the receipts and their contents. [Minting](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/go/serve.go#L487), [admission, release and sealing](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/go/serve.go#L819), [seal format and bound](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md#L350)

This is a terminology gap, not evidence that the implementation incorrectly counts admissions.

**Smallest edit:** Replace the sentence with:

> Under the base proposal, the seal authenticates the signer’s declared final count of minted report receipts, whose contents are checked through store verification. It establishes neither a count of submissions merely received or admitted nor that every routed call was reported.

## Findings (verbatim)

## Verdict: `mergeable after fixes`

The central distinction is defensible: the engine can attest **receipt of an authenticated report**, without attesting that the reported call happened. But several sentences exceed that bound, and important lifecycle and consumer questions are missing.

Reviewed [RFC 0015](../0015-reported-receipts.md), SHA-256 `699b03d3a294fb7d3af77eee9808f61beb6525da007084433a5a761755e506b3`, against gateway `9e9ac55` and runtime `0c8a4b6`, using their `origin/main` objects. No files changed.

### 1. MAJOR — The kind-versus-shape argument omits an existing mandatory check

**Sentence, lines 155–157:**

> A report filed under `kind: "acquisition"` with a new `shape` would pass wherever an acquisition is accepted unless every consumer also checks `shape`, and nothing written so far obliges one to.

**Source/problem:** Gateway §1.4 order 1 expressly rejects **both** an unknown `kind` and an unknown acquisition `shape` as `malformed`. A consumer following §5a cannot accept the latter through an unchanged conforming verifier. A third kind remains a reasonable semantic distinction, but old-verifier refusal does not distinguish these alternatives. After updating verification, application policy might overlook either discriminator. [Gateway SPEC](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md)

**Smallest edit:** State that both additions fail existing version-3 structural verification. Justify `kind` by the different attestation semantics and the desired consumer policy boundary; describe kind-only application checks as a **post-upgrade** concern.

### 2. MAJOR — Compatibility overstates whole-store withholding and misstates versioning’s effect

**Sentences, lines 292–294 and 353–354:**

> A new `kind` is outside every version 3 verifier's enumeration: such a verifier reports a report as `malformed`, and by §5a.1 withholds every session of a store that holds one.

> Whether a receipt version change (clause 11) is the right way to keep old verifiers from withholding a store for a kind they could not know.

**Source/problem:** The first statement holds for a report presented **as version 3**, under the default store-wide consumer rule. §5a.1 also permits deliberate session-scoped consumption, subject to its complete conditions—including global record-citation failures.

A version bump does not make an old verifier accept the store. The pinned implementation skips version-specific structural checks for unknown versions and returns `unsupported-version` when common structural checks pass. Consequently, Conformance’s unconditional expectation of `malformed` for the previous verifier is also wrong if version 4 is chosen. [SPEC §§1.4, 5a.1](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md), [verifier implementation](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/go/verify.go)

**Smallest edit:** Distinguish:

- version-3 unknown kind → `malformed`;
- otherwise well-formed unknown version → `unsupported-version`;
- default store-wide refusal versus permitted session-scoped consumption.

Replace Unresolved 9 with a migration question. Versioning identifies incompatibility; it does not remove it. Any store separation alternative must also address the registry’s expected session set.

### 3. MAJOR — Acquisition provenance is generalized beyond the source

**Sentence, lines 35–37:**

> In both, the program that touched the outside world was the engine's own: named by the operator, pinned by digest in the catalog, and run under the isolation the operator establishes.

**Source/problem:** Catalog-backed engine platforms have that deployment discipline. The gateway format also admits bare `command` acquisitions and operator-declared adapter subprocesses. For a command, the gateway records a digest of the resolved executable; that is not a catalog pin, and an interpreter’s digest does not bind its script. For adapter shapes, adapter identity is envelope testimony. Isolation remains conditional. [SPEC §1.2a](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md), [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SECURITY.md)

The related claim that the reporter is a party the engine “did not choose” also conflicts with clause 3’s proposed operator allowlist. Selection, pinning, execution and attribution are different properties.

**Smallest edit:** Qualify the catalog statement as describing the catalog-backed engine path. State the general distinction as **engine-started source versus remotely submitted account**, with configuration and isolation assumptions explicit. Replace “did not choose” with “did not itself run or establish the executing program’s identity.”

### 4. MAJOR — Reporter authentication lacks a transport question

**Sentence, lines 179–180:**

> A reporter would authenticate as any caller does — a bearer token verified against the engine's configured issuer, audience and key set — and be recorded as `reporter`.

**Source/problem:** Token verification does not authenticate the engine to the reporter or protect the submission and response. The current reference uses localhost plaintext HTTP; its security document explicitly makes salt custody dependent on transport. The existing plugin note even documents a certificate-verification problem that could expose a bearer to an impersonating engine. [Gateway security boundary](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SECURITY.md), [plugins note](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/plugins.md)

**Smallest edit:** Add an unresolved transport clause: how the reporter authenticates the engine, where confidentiality and integrity terminate, which components can read bearer tokens and salts, and how credentials are renewed or revoked. Distinguish this transport from the reporter’s claimed upstream `peerIdentity`. No particular transport mechanism needs choosing here.

### 5. MAJOR — `caller` and `reporter` are undefined for relayed user calls

**Sentence, lines 180–182:**

> What the token proves is the gateway's existing statement about `caller`: who submitted, at the boundary.

**Source/problem:** A gateway reporting a user’s call introduces at least two identities: the submitting reporter and the original requester. With the gateway’s service token, the authenticated principal is the gateway. With a forwarded user token, the authenticated principal is the user; that token does not independently authenticate the reporting plugin.

The existing MCP-server note states this distinction explicitly and rejects tokens for another resource. RFC 0015 proposes `reporter` without specifying its relationship to the existing top-level `caller`. [MCP-server identity section](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/mcp-server.md)

**Smallest edit:** Ask whether `caller` duplicates the authenticated submitter, whether original-requester identity is carried separately as reporter testimony, and what evidence would establish delegation if stronger attribution is intended. Do not imply one bearer independently proves both roles.

### 6. MAJOR — The reported object and observation point need their own contract

**Sentences, lines 113 and 139–140:**

> A reporter is a `tool_post_invoke` hook.

> The engine vouches for R's identity as its token verification establishes it, for S, for the report's bytes, and for their position.

**Source/problem:** Naming a hook does not establish which request/result pair it observes or which bytes ultimately reach the user. Other hooks can modify payloads. Concurrent calls need correlation; errors, cancellation and transport failures may supply different objects—or no answer.

The gateway signs canonical retained values, not arbitrary original wire bytes. Its canonical domain excludes floating-point literals, while MCP results can contain structured content, error flags and resource links. A reported link does not attest subsequently fetched content. [SPEC §§1.1, 6](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md), [MCP tool results](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/server/tools)

**Smallest edit:** Add questions defining:

- the observation point relative to routing and payload modification;
- call/result correlation and success/error/unknown coverage;
- the exact retained representation and treatment of values outside the canonical domain;
- which report members are engine-derived versus reporter assertions.

Narrow “the report’s bytes” accordingly. This does not require re-verifying ContextForge now.

### 7. MAJOR — Session separation does not settle sealing, delivery or registry compatibility

**Sentences, lines 192–194 and 297–298:**

> A candidate rule: a session holds reports or it holds acquisitions and actions, never both.

> The seal and the registry are unchanged; the corpus would gain vectors.

**Source/problem:** Kind separation answers neither who can append nor who can seal. Existing sessions share a namespace; callers knowing a name can share and seal it. Transport closure does not seal, and signer restart can leave unsealed sessions unverifiable. Remote reporting adds reports still queued outside the signer when sealing occurs. A sealed count establishes accepted receipts, never that every routed call was reported. [MCP-server sessions](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/mcp-server.md), [SPEC §§3–4](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md)

Moreover, the separate-report-key alternative cannot simply assume unchanged verification: the current registry loader drops seals under another key.

**Smallest edit:** Make unchanged seal/registry **formats conditional**, and add lifecycle questions: append/seal authority, sharing among reporters, in-flight submissions, lost acknowledgements and retries, late reports, crash recovery, and registry handling under separate keys or stores. Explicitly disclaim completeness of the underlying call history.

### 8. MAJOR — Retention, deletion and correction are absent

**Sentence, lines 313–315:**

> Its `statement` and arguments would be committed as an acquisition's are, and its result retained in the clear as any artifact is; what a reporter puts in the other members is in the receipt in the clear.

**Source/problem:** Disclosure is identified, but its lifecycle is not. Correcting or removing a submitted report is consequential: rewriting breaks signatures and chains; deleting a retained result produces `artifact-missing`; removing sealed receipts or sessions conflicts with the registry. Artifacts may also be shared by digest. [Gateway SPEC](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md)

**Smallest edit:** Add unresolved questions about retention, authorized deletion, erroneous-report correction or retraction, and what a consumer concludes afterward. State that deletion and correction cannot be assumed verification-neutral. Leave the mechanism unchosen.

### 9. MAJOR — The time window is described as evidence of actual freshness

**Sentence, lines 202–204:**

> A candidate: refuse the first, bound the second by configuration, and record the window the engine applied, so a consumer can tell a report received within a minute of the call it describes from one received a week later.

**Design problem:** The engine compares its clock with a reporter-selected timestamp. A week-old—or invented—call can be reported with a current `observedAt`. The proposed check distinguishes claimed observation times, not actual call ages. Zero tolerance for a future timestamp also needs a clock-skew question.

**Smallest edit:** Replace the conclusion with “so a consumer can tell which window the **claimed observation time** satisfied.” Add clock skew and the definition of the engine’s receipt timestamp to Unresolved questions. A fabricated fresh timestamp should be an expected-undetectable case.

### 10. MAJOR — An upstream signature needs binding semantics, and does not create a universal assurance ranking

**Sentence, lines 216–218:**

> If the endpoint itself signed its answer, under a key the operator pins for that endpoint, a report could carry that signature as `upstreamToken`, and a verifier could check it: the bytes would then be the endpoint's by the endpoint's own statement, whatever the reporter is.

**Design problem:** This is defensible only for the bytes actually covered by that signature. It does not establish that the answer belongs to the reported request, requester, invocation or time. A genuine signed answer can be replayed under another reported call. Canonicalization or transformations can also change the signed object.

The existing `upstreamToken` is an opaque carried string, not a specified validation protocol. Absence from a report cannot establish that the endpoint offered no proof; the reporter might omit it. [SPEC §1.2a](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md)

**Smallest edit:** Ask what the upstream signature covers, how it binds the relevant call context, how trust and key lifecycle work, and what missing, invalid or unchecked tokens mean. Narrow the benefit to an authenticated endpoint statement. Remove the categorical “only clause”/“approach an acquisition’s bound” ranking: independently authenticated origin and engine-mediated acquisition are different properties.

### 11. MAJOR — Citation changes need an assertion boundary and explicit verdict semantics

**Sentence, lines 230–234:**

> Candidates: the action ladder refuses a citation of a report unless the operator's configuration allows it for that platform; the verifier's join resolves it and reports a distinct status (for instance `citation-reported`) that a consumer can refuse; the runtime's citation rule records the cited receipt's kind beside its signature.

**Source/problem:** ADR-0033 deliberately makes the runtime record caller-supplied citations without reading a receipt or store. It therefore cannot independently obtain the receipt’s kind. A supplied kind remains an assertion requiring comparison with the authenticated receipt. Current runtime input also requires exactly three citation members; adding a fourth changes that grammar. [Runtime ADR-0033](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-runtime/blob/0c8a4b6/docs/adr/0033-a-record-cites-the-receipts-it-relied-on.md), [citation parser](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-runtime/blob/0c8a4b6/internal/audit/audit.go)

Separately, current citation findings affect store-wide `ok`; record findings have no session and require explicit treatment under session scoping. Calling something a new “status” leaves its acceptance effect unspecified.

**Smallest edit:** State that any runtime-recorded kind would be caller-asserted unless the runtime’s boundary changes. Ask whether kind should instead be derived during verification, how disagreement is handled, and whether report citation is informational or refusing under each consumer policy. Record the grammar and migration consequence.

### 12. MAJOR — Reporter trust and result hashing are insufficient consumer binding

**Sentence, lines 239–244:**

> A consumer rule in the manner of §5a would state: a consumer that requires the lineage of an acquisition refuses `kind: "report"`; a report verifies as a report and does not change the store-wide verdict by being one; binding a report (§5a.4) means binding its `reporter` to a principal the consumer trusts for that endpoint as well as re-digesting its result — the consumer's trust in a report is its trust in the reporter, and the engine's signature adds the time and the position, not the truth.

**Design problem:** A valid report by the expected reporter about the expected endpoint can still concern another query, user or invocation. Its signature and result digest will verify. The existing MCP consumer guidance explicitly compares signed source, session, caller and opened commitments with the intended request. [MCP-server consumer guidance](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/mcp-server.md)

**Smallest edit:** Preserve the complete §5a.4 ceremony and add binding to the intended reported call and relevant context. Ask who receives and retains commitment salts across the relay, and how a consumer distinguishes another genuine report for the same reporter/endpoint.

### 13. MAJOR — The proxy alternative claims more coverage than the implementation supplies

**Sentences, lines 274–278:**

> Configure each server the MCP gateway routes as an engine platform, and let the engine's own MCP adapter make the call through `gateway mcp`.

> Every call is then an acquisition, honest by construction.

**Source/problem:** The current MCP adapter starts a **stdio** server from a pinned image or local command; it is not a general client for every remotely hosted MCP endpoint. The frontend exposes configured live tools, omits `/act` and other MCP capabilities, and receipts successful acquisitions—not every failed invocation. Moving a call can also change credentials, state and behavior. [MCP adapter](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/adapters/mcp/adapter.go), [MCP-server design](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/mcp-server.md)

Thus its cost is not established as merely configuration. Nor does an engine fronting a server imply that a particular independently routed call went through the engine.

**Smallest edit:** Limit the alternative to calls compatible with the current platform/adapter path. Name transport bridging and semantic changes as open costs. Replace “Every call” with “Each successful acquisition through that path.” Similarly narrow Summary’s “only ever” and Unresolved 1’s “any server” language.

### 14. MAJOR — Notarization does not eliminate laundering

**Sentence, lines 281–283:**

> This moves the laundering risk out of the engine's signature entirely, at the cost of a key per reporter.

**Design problem:** A consumer can still mistake the engine’s countersignature for endorsement of the enclosed claim, ignore the reporter’s signature, or misread the signed object’s purpose. The proposed ordinary report already distinguishes the reporter’s claim from the engine’s assertion that it received it.

Reporter signatures offer a real additional property: independently checkable attribution to the reporter’s key. They also introduce trust distribution and key-lifecycle questions.

**Smallest edit:** Replace “entirely” with a conditional benefit: notarization can make attribution independently verifiable and reduce confusion **when consumers verify and distinguish both statements**. Retain laundering as a consumer-contract risk and expand the stated cost beyond key count.

### 15. MINOR — The flooding comparison is unsupported

**Sentence, lines 249–250:**

> That makes a report surface cheaper to flood than `/acquire`, whose cost is bounded by the sources' own deadlines and output bounds.

**Source/problem:** Eliminating a source run lowers server work per request; it does not establish comparative attacker cost or sustainable flooding throughput. Source deadlines and output limits bound individual runs, not aggregate concurrency or retained storage. The MCP-server note expressly says signer acquisitions are admitted without a global bound. [Bounds discussion](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/mcp-server.md)

**Smallest edit:** Say reporting has a different resource profile: attacker-selected input directly drives parsing, signing and retention. Add questions about aggregate concurrency, pending work, session/registry growth and verification cost alongside the existing quotas.

### 16. MINOR — The witness alternative turns a conditional aim into a property

**Sentence, lines 284–286:**

> Reporters append to a log that independent witnesses cosign, in the sense of [RFC 0012](../0012-witness-contract.md): the property is detection of a split view, not attestation of a call, and it answers a different question.

**Source/problem:** RFC 0012 expressly disclaims general split-view detection. Its clauses concern whether authenticated conflicting views reach a comparator, coverage, retention, enforcement and non-collusion. It does not prescribe one log-cosigning mechanism. Witnessing could also complement reported receipts rather than replace them.

**Smallest edit:** Say the alternative *aims to make conflicting histories observable under a specified witness contract*, without establishing call truth. Identify it as potentially complementary. The terminology distinction and Evidence’s broader analogy to RFC 0012 are otherwise accurate.

### 17. MINOR — The summary reverses its intended subject

**Sentence, lines 52–54:**

> The strongest form the project has, an acquisition, must never be readable as one; most of the clauses exist to keep it so.

**Problem:** Grammatically, “one” refers to a reported receipt: the sentence protects acquisitions from being read as reports, reversing the main laundering concern.

**Smallest edit:** “A reported receipt must never be readable as an acquisition; most of the clauses exist to keep it so.”

## Checks that passed

- **Process and scope:** All ten section headings are present. The draft status, hypothetical implementation/conformance claims, repository ownership and acceptance-as-record-only language follow the exploratory precedents. Nothing purports to land in JPS.
- **Index:** The filename, title and Draft status match RFC 0015.
- **Quoted sources:** The adapter-testimony passage, §4 step-5 quotation, caller-receipt prohibitions and MCP coverage quotation exist as cited. Citation resolution really is blind to the target’s kind; its verification remains a separate finding.
- **ContextForge:** The hook names and modes match the gateway’s plugins note. RFC 0015 honestly limits that evidence to the note.
- **MCP signature claim:** The client proposes `2025-06-18`, while also accepting `2025-03-26` and `2024-11-05`. The proposed revision defines no standard result-signature field or verification contract; extensible result data does not prohibit application-specific signatures. [MCP schema](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/schema)
- **PR 121:** It merged as `9e9ac55`, and its discussion records six implementation-review rounds. [PR 121](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/pull/121)

**Required fixes:** Address findings **1–17**. For the missing design work, recording the question, its consequence and its owning repository is sufficient; this review does not require choosing or building the mechanism.

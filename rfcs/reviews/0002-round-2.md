1. **R2-1 — MAJOR: the retained Study 004 paragraph still overstates the evidence and calls the scope choice open.**

   Exact sentences, RFC lines 97–104:

   > The measured observation this leaves for the RFC to weigh, not a resolution: within one policy's own decisions, the cross-decision escape in this corpus is effect/entitlement-shaped rather than dataflow-shaped, while the outcome-as-input edge this sketch proposes matches the seam *between* systems — an upstream decision's recorded verdict consumed as a downstream document's fact — which that study's frame, by construction, could not contain.

   > Whether this RFC scopes composition to the between-systems seam or grows the edge toward entitlement constructs is now an evidenced choice, and still an open one.

   **What the source says:** RFC lines 161–169 explicitly decide the dataflow scope and say nothing here demonstrates the edge’s need or sufficiency between owners. Lines 174–177 distinguish judgment from measurement. The retained paragraph still presents the between-systems match within “The measured observation,” despite acknowledging that this case was excluded from the frame, and then contradicts the decision with “still an open one.”

   **Smallest edit:** retain the within-policy observation, but strike or explicitly supersede the categorical between-systems clause and the open-choice sentence with a dated qualification: the between-systems use is intended and unmeasured; Scope now selects dataflow and excludes effect/entitlement constructs. Apply the same explicit supersession treatment used for F1.

2. **R2-2 — MINOR: the new inheritance sentence mischaracterizes the original scope of all three decisions.**

   Exact sentence, RFC lines 112–114:

   > The list is not everything that governs the surface: it also inherits the runtime's decisions on the reviewed-set lock, audit records and evaluation traces, which were made for single packs and apply to graphs as well.

   **What the sources say:** application to graphs is correct, but the single-pack origin characterization is misleading for each decision:

   - **Evaluation traces:** ADR-0027’s original scope expressly binds standalone evaluations **and graph node evaluations**: `docs/adr/0027-pin-the-evaluation-trace-contract.md:90–99`; runtime `CHANGELOG.md:193–207`.

   These were decisions covering both surfaces, not merely single-pack decisions subsequently applicable to graphs.

   **Smallest edit:** delete “, which were made for single packs and apply to graphs as well”. The remaining sentence accurately identifies additional governing decisions.

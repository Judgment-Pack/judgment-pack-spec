**N1 — MAJOR — The byte-identity qualification leaves an unauthorized exception reading available.**

R:196–198:

> “The byte-identity requirement of §8.3 extends to `value`, for the inputs two conforming implementations both admit.”

Core §8.2 requires agreement on admission (`spec/judgment-pack-core.md:593–610`). Section 8.3 requires identical canonical dispositions for identical inputs and names only the arithmetic exception within §§7–8 (670–679). Section 10 separately excludes inputs exceeding documented implementation limits (777–779).

Actual mutual admission is not itself another authorized exception. Read together with those requirements, the qualifier is redundant and cannot license arbitrary refusal. Read as the proposed extension’s scope restriction, however, it exempts the very admission disagreement that *Resolution* correctly says has not been established as conforming.

Thus the five related passages do not yet express one unambiguous contract. Describing the admission question as unresolved is appropriate; narrowing the portable requirement by whatever implementations happen to admit is not a substitute for resolving its scope.

**Smallest edit:** Restore “The byte-identity requirement of §8.3 extends to `value`.” State that question 9 introduces no additional exception pending an explicit Core decision reconciled with §§8.2–8.4 and §10.

**N2 — MINOR — The three proposed answers conflate carrier validity, admission policy, and portable scope.**

R:227–230:

> “It is one of three things: not a carrier-conforming text, so that every conforming evaluator refuses it; a text an implementation may refuse under a documented limit, as §10 treats sizes, so that such an input is outside the portable claim; or a text every conforming evaluator admits.”

R:230–231:

> “This proposal's row for such a fact has one answer under the third, another under the first, and is outside the claim under the second.”

These are not an exhaustive set of specification choices **as formulated**. Core could retain RFC 8259 carrier validity while imposing a uniform restriction specifically on facts admission. Section 8.2 already distinguishes carrier validity from additional input requirements for evidence documents. Core could also define a mandatory supported domain and permit restrictions outside it.

The consequences need qualification:

- Required facts refusal gives `malformed-input`, assuming pack validation succeeds.
- Required admission gives this selected-value row `unresolved`/`unknown`, assuming other preflight checks and limits permit evaluation.
- Optional documented restrictions do not, merely by existing, remove every such input from portability. Section 10 excludes an input **above either implementation’s limit**. An otherwise valid input admitted within both limits does not lose its guarantee on that account. A broader exclusion would need an explicit Core decision.

**Smallest edit:** Introduce these as possible approaches, separate carrier validity from facts-admission restrictions, and specify the excluded domain rather than deriving it from permission to impose limits. Replace “the three answers Core could give” at R:523–524 accordingly.

**N3 — MINOR — The scope correction still overstates which evaluations change.**

> “The need is not of this proposal's making: it holds for any pack evaluated over such a document.”

R:522–523:

> “The question is Core's and is wider than this proposal: it decides the answer for any conforming pack evaluated over such a facts document.”

Core §8.2:593–597 and §8.4:725–733 require pack validation first. A nonconforming pack produces `pack-not-conformant` regardless of facts admission. The new Core-needs item reintroduces the unqualified “any pack” scope.

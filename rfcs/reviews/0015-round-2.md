## 2. Additional findings

### R2-1 — MAJOR: The consumer rule loses the reporter-trust requirement

**Exact sentence, lines 327–331:**

> Stated in terms of the kind (clause 3): a consumer that requires the lineage of an acquisition refuses `kind: "report"`; a report verifies as a report and does not change the store-wide verdict by being one; and a consumer that accepts a report accepts the reporter's account under clause 12's binding — the engine's signature adds the reporter's verified identity, the time of receipt and the position, not the truth of the account.

**Problem:** Clause 12 establishes correspondence with the expected report and intended call. It no longer explicitly requires a consumer decision to trust the authenticated reporting principal for that endpoint. Clause 4’s operator allowlist establishes permission to submit, which is a different decision.

A correctly bound, valid report can still come from a principal the consumer does not trust as evidence. Round 1 asked to add binding requirements to the existing trust requirement; the replacement loses that requirement.

**Smallest edit:** Add to clause 13:

> A consumer relying on the account must explicitly trust the authenticated reporting principal for that endpoint; the engine’s admission allowlist does not establish the consumer’s trust.

### R2-2 — MAJOR: An action receipt does not establish that a write was performed

**Exact sentence, lines 34–35:**

> An *acquisition* attests bytes a source returned; an *action* is the lineage of a write the engine performed.

**Source:** §1.2a defines an action as the request to an executor and the target’s response. A target’s refusal is also receipted: the executor uses `--error-results`. Consequently, a valid action receipt need not represent a write that took effect. RFC 0014 §4 already makes this distinction. [Gateway SPEC](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md#L216), [executor design](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/executor.md#L134)

**Smallest edit:**

> An acquisition attests bytes a source returned; an action records an authenticated write request made through the engine’s executor and the target’s response, including a refusal.

Likewise narrow line 94’s “the engine acquired or did” to acquisition or a mediated request and response.

### R2-3 — MINOR: Restart loses recovery state; it does not prohibit every later write or seal under the name

**Exact sentence, lines 250–253:**

> Today sessions share one namespace, any caller who knows a name can append to it and seal it, a transport session's end seals nothing, and a signer restart empties the signer's session map, so a session left unsealed at a restart can be neither continued nor sealed by the process that follows.

**Source:** The empty map and immediate `/seal` refusal are correct. But `/acquire` can create a new in-memory entry for an old name. With an intact `0.json`, stamping fails on the exclusive write; if that receipt is missing, an acquisition can recreate it and advance the new counter. The existing `act_test.go` explicitly exercises this case. `sealSession` subsequently uses the in-memory counter without requiring that this process created the session directory. [Acquisition and sealing code](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/go/serve.go#L819), [existing test](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/go/act_test.go#L299)

This is **not** recovery of the previous history, and does not establish a valid reconstructed store. It does refute the categorical prohibition.

**Smallest edit:** Replace the restart conclusion with:

> …so the process that follows restores neither the previous count nor the previous chain; `/seal` refuses a session absent from its in-memory map rather than reconstructing it from disk.

### R2-4 — MINOR: Rewriting does not break every subsequent chain link

**Exact sentence, lines 337–340:**

> What happens afterwards is unspecified and consequential: rewriting a receipt breaks its signature and every later link of its chain; deleting a retained result makes the receipt `artifact-missing`; removing sealed receipts or sessions conflicts with the registry; and an artifact may be shared by digest with another receipt.

**Source:** Changing signed values without re-signing invalidates that receipt’s signature. Later receipts’ signatures and adjacent `prevSignature` comparisons do not all thereby change. The verifier excludes failed receipts and checks sequence before chain, so a missing passing receipt can produce `sequence-broken`, with no chain walk. If the edited receipt is re-signed, its immediate successor’s previous-signature reference becomes stale. [SPEC §1.4](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md#L299), [verification implementation](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/go/verify.go#L622)

**Smallest edit:** Replace the first consequence with:

> changing a receipt’s signed values without re-signing invalidates its signature and can prevent reconstruction of the session

This also avoids implying that a whitespace-only rewrite changes the canonical signed input.

### R2-5 — MINOR: The fabrication impossibility statement needs a baseline qualification

**Exact sentence, lines 274–276:**

> This record does not propose a mechanism against fabrication, because there is none at the engine: the engine did not see the call.

**Design problem:** Clause 10 leaves independently authenticated endpoint evidence open, including evidence binding the request and invocation. Requiring and validating such evidence could reject some reporter fabrications or substitutions without the engine having made the call.

That would still authenticate an endpoint’s statement, not establish objective truth. The baseline limitation is sound; the categorical claim that no mechanism can counter fabrication is broader than it, and sits uneasily beside clause 10.

**Smallest edit:**

> From the reporter’s account alone, the engine cannot establish whether the call occurred. Whether clause 10’s independently authenticated evidence would constrain acceptance remains open.

### R2-6 — MINOR: Domain separation does not protect against “any reading”

**Exact sentence, lines 201–204:**

> `kind` is already inside the signed bytes, so a report cannot be re-labelled without breaking its signature; a separate prefix would additionally keep a report's signature from being valid as any other signed object's under any reading, at the cost of one more rule every verifier implements.

**Design problem:** A distinct prefix separates specified signing domains when verifiers enforce their respective prefixes. It cannot constrain another protocol that reuses the same signing input or a consumer that assigns the verified statement another meaning. The gateway specification makes the narrower, concrete claim about its receipt and seal domains. [Receipt coverage](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md#L240), [seal coverage](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/SPEC.md#L350)

**Smallest edit:** Replace “any other signed object's under any reading” with:

> a receipt or seal whose verifier requires its different specified prefix

## 3. Whole-record checks

- **Process and ownership:** All ten RFC 0000 sections are present. Draft status, hypothetical conformance and implementation language, repository ownership, and acceptance-as-record-only semantics are honest.
- **Registration and citations:** The index and page registration agree with the record. Referenced local documents and named gateway/runtime sections exist. PR 121 is merged and its discussion records six implementation-review rounds. [PR 121](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/pull/121)
- **Unsupported versions:** `receiptFrom` skips version-specific checks for unknown versions; common structural and lexical failures still precede `unsupported-version`. Clause 16’s qualification is sufficient. [Verifier](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/go/verify.go#L77)
- **Registry:** The stated `keyId` check is exact. Signature verification is additionally required; a matching identifier alone is insufficient. [Registry loader](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/go/verify.go#L294)
- **Certificate passage:** The note concerns the pinned Activepieces client setting `NODE_TLS_REJECT_UNAUTHORIZED=0`. The reference piece explicitly requires certificate verification on each connection. RFC 0015 neither claims the reference piece disables verification nor re-verifies today’s framework. [Plugins note](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/docs/design/plugins.md#L63)
- **MCP reach and signatures:** The revised coverage statement matches the adapter/frontend. The client proposes `2025-06-18`; that schema provides extensible results but no standard result-signature contract. [Adapter](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-gateway/blob/9e9ac55/adapters/mcp/adapter.go#L78), [MCP schema](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/schema#calltoolresult)
- **Clause consistency:** Clause 11’s potentially refusing *citation finding* does not contradict clause 13’s otherwise valid *report receipt*: they concern different acceptance conditions. Conditional key/store variants likewise do not contradict the base proposal. R2-5 is the remaining scope mismatch between clauses.

## Verdict: `mergeable after fixes`

**Two MAJOR and four MINOR findings; no BLOCKER.**

Required fixes: **R2-1 through R2-6**—restore consumer trust, correct the action bound, narrow restart behavior, correct chain mechanics, qualify the fabrication claim, and bound domain separation.

These require edits to the exploratory record, not selection or implementation of a reporting mechanism.

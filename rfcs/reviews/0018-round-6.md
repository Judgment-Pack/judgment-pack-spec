## Findings

### M1 — MEDIUM — The form of a research acquisition, and the value the read starts from, are not stated; §5.2's exact form allows two readings

Where: `spec/dialect.md`:351-355 (§4.3), :828-831 (§11, "Replaying research") and :845 (the `sources.json` row). These conflict with :462 (§5.2) and :869-871 (refusal phase 5). The RFC has the same text at `rfcs/0018-dialect.md`:635-639, :1112-1115, :1129 and :746.

What happens: §5.2 requires each source's member to be `{"response": {"result": R, "receipt": E, "salts": S}}` "exactly": one result, one receipt, one salts object. The amendment says a research source's acquisition "is the retained response set … each named by its digest and held by its receipt". It also says the research record "retains that set for the source's read (§6)". That allows two readings:

- **(a) The set sits beside the one response, or replaces it.** §5.2's "exactly" then refuses it at phase 5 as `input-malformed`, so the new `acquisition-invalid` can never be reported.
- **(b) The set sits inside R, the research record.** R is then one gateway answer, which §5.2 checks under one receipt. No member name says where a response, its digest, its receipt or its salts sit in R. The RFC's Unresolved question 3 (:1511) still leaves R's form to the Desk (#369).

The value read is also unstated. §6.1 starts from R. §11 says the derivation is replayed "over the retained responses", and that a bad response is refused "before its value is read", as if each response's value were read. A source has one claim (§6), and the two readings produce different preparations.

Confirmed by running. `.review-scratch/probe_set.py` runs the repository's first reader on copies of the `valid` vector (log: `.review-scratch/probe-set.log`):

| Variant | What the reader gave |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline, unchanged | a reading |
| A `retained` member beside `result`, `receipt` and `salts` | refusal `input-malformed` |
| An array of responses in place of the member | refusal `input-malformed` |
| The set nested inside R | `acquisition-invalid` (resultDigest) |

So reading (b) works only when the source's own receipt signs the research record with its set.

Fix: state the form in §5.2 for a `research` source. For example, R with named members such as `responses`, an array of `{result, receipt, salts, request}`, or a member beside `response`. Then say the read applies to R. Otherwise, say plainly that the form, and with it the rule's mechanics, wait on #369 and the first preparer, and mark the rule "(No vector decides this.)".

### M2 — MEDIUM — "A retained response whose digest or receipt does not hold": which checks apply, and under which profile

Where: `spec/dialect.md`:830-831 and :354-355; in the RFC, :1114-1115 and :638-639. These conflict with `spec/dialect.md`:326 ("Each is acquired through the gateway source its profile pins, so §5's checks apply"), :480-486 (checks 3 and 4), :487-497 (times and sessions) and :887-891 (the findings' order).

What happens: the "(§5.2)" reference implies gateway version-3 acquisition receipts, checked as §5.2 checks one. But §5.2's checks are tied to "its source and its profile": check 2's key is the profile's `publicKey`; check 3 requires `E.source`, `E.authority` and `E.acquisition.adapter`/`shape`/`endpoint` to equal the profile's; check 4 opens the commitment over "the source's request".

A web read made during the run either came through another gateway source, or was made by the provider and has no gateway receipt at all. Through the gateway it would be the web source: `adapter-web`, shape `http` (§4.3). Its receipt then fails check 3 under the research profile. Every answer after the first, and every web read, was made with a request other than the source's, so check 4 cannot be computed for them. A verifier MUST NOT take a profile from an acquisition (§5.1), and no text names the profiles the web reads are checked against.

"Holds" therefore reads two ways: **(i) Checks 1–5 under the research profile.** No fixture with a web read can then verify. **(ii) The signature and `resultDigest` only.** The verifier must then pick a key and drop checks 3 and 4.

Three more things are not said: whether `acquisition-stale` (`maxAge` against `verifiedAt`) applies to each response; whether `acquisition-sessions` applies to each response; whether, for N responses, the finding codes run per source or per response. Per source, every response's `acquisition-invalid` comes before any `acquisition-stale`. The two orders report different findings when response 1 is stale and response 2 is invalid.

Fix: for each retained response, state: which §5.2 checks apply; which trusted profile it is checked against (for example, the record names a profile id and digest per response, and that profile must be among the trusted ones); which request its commitment opens to, and where those bytes are kept; whether times and sessions apply; the order of findings within one source.

### M3 — MEDIUM — The preparation's `cites`, and a lineage entry's `receipt`, for a source with many receipts

Where: `spec/dialect.md`:698 (§9.1 `cites`), :736 (§9.3 `receipt`) and :896-900 (finding 6). The amendment changes none of them.

What happens: a preparation carries "every acquisition's citation", and each lineage entry carries "the acquisition's citation" as one object. A research source has one receipt per retained response. Under reading (b) of M1 it also has the research record's own. `cites` could list every retained receipt's citation, only the record's, or both. That choice decides the preparation's canonical bytes: a verifier that chooses differently from the preparer reports `preparation-differs`; the record's `cites` comparison reports `record-inputs-differ`. The single `receipt` member of a lineage entry has the same problem.

Fix: state which citations a research source adds to `cites`, and which one its lineage entries carry.

### M4 — MEDIUM — Missing, extra and reordered responses, and who assembles the set

Where: `spec/dialect.md`:351-359, including "A research record that did not come back through a gateway source is no acquisition", and :828-831. In the RFC: :635-643 and :1112-1115.

What happens: `acquisition-missing` and `acquisition-unexpected` work per source member (§5.2). Nothing says what a verifier reports when: the research record names a response, by digest, that is not retained; a retained response is one the record does not name; the order differs.

The quoted sentence now reads two ways: **(a) The gateway assembled the set.** The record came back as a whole under one receipt. That receipt commits membership and order, so a reordered set fails check 5. **(b) The caller assembled the set.** Issue #161 describes this: the Desk's research run builds the record from responses that each came back through a gateway source. Then nothing signed commits membership or order, and the selection is only the caller's word. By the sentence's own wording, such a record is "no acquisition" and enters only as an asserted file.

Fix: say who produces the research record and what commits the set. If the caller does, say that membership and order are asserted. State the finding for a missing, an extra and a reordered response, and say it in §13 too (see L3).

### M5 — MEDIUM — "Compared as text": against what, in what form, and with what finding

Where: `spec/dialect.md`:356-357 and :832-833. In the RFC: :640-641, :1116-1117, :205-206 and :1569. These are checked against `spec/dialect.md`:346-350 (the recipe's members) and :428-440 (§5.1's closed profile).

What happens: the recipe is in the dialect, and "compared as text" needs something to compare it with. The text names nothing. There are three candidates: **the receipt's commitment (check 4)**, which compares `canon` bytes, not text; **a copy of the recipe in the research record**, not signed except through R's digest; **the profile**, which has no model member (§5.1 is closed). Fix: name what is compared and in what form. For example: "the recipe is the request, and check 4 holds the receipt's commitment to its `canon` bytes". Then name the finding, or write "recorded" instead of "compared". List the members as §4.3 names them. Say that the model's name is not checked against anything a verifier is given.

### M6 — MEDIUM — A web read cited by URL, with no receipt

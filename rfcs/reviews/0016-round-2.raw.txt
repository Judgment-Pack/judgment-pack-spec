**B. What I examined and found sound**

- **Placement and schema changes.** The schema provides `extensions` on nine object kinds: root, decision, evidence requirement, source, outcome, rule, exception, escalation, and metadata. The universal restriction covers every non-outcome site; listing them individually is unnecessary. Both reserved-prefix predicates are correctly identified at schema lines 109 and 417. In-memory checks reproduced both current rejections and confirmed that admitting the name structurally still leaves semantic placement validation necessary.

- **Strings and decimals.** Constants and selected facts consistently use Unicode scalar-value sequences. RFC 8785 §3.2.2.2 requires an error for lone surrogates, so the canonicalization explanation is correct. A valid escaped surrogate pair represents an admitted supplementary-plane character. Empty strings and whitespace remain valid `string` values. Core §2.2’s decimal grammar already excludes surrogates and every other non-ASCII character; decimal needs no additional restriction.

- **Value names.** The whole-name rule is unambiguous. Its mandatory first lowercase ASCII letter excludes an empty member name. Subsequent uppercase ASCII letters and digits are permitted; initial capitals, initial digits, and trailing line feeds are excluded.

- **Preflight and error precedence.** For a supporting evaluator, malformed declarations are pack errors under the proposed semantic constraints, including declarations on unselected outcomes. Core §8.2 requires pack validation before facts, evidence availability, capability checks, and step 1. Consequently, `pack-not-conformant` precedes false applicability and a competing malformed evidence document, as §8.4 requires. R2-2 concerns the paragraph’s contradictory premise, not that precedence.

  For an evaluator that **does not support the extension**, an otherwise Core-valid pack requiring it reaches `unsupported-required-extension`, even when the opaque contents violate extension-specific declaration rules. The proposal scopes those validation obligations to supporting consumers; Compatibility, lines 279–283, supplies the unsupported-consumer result. The combined unsupported-and-malformed case is not stated directly as a conformance row. Independently detectable Core nonconformance or malformed input still takes precedence.

- **Resolution and handoff.** Steps 6, 9, and 10 are every Core outcome exit. Resolution cannot alter a non-outcome result. Failed value resolution produces only `unknown`, omits both `outcomeId` and `value`, and cannot retry fallback. A preliminary outcome has no retained blocking reasons; direct exception escalation would already have terminated at step 5. Thus handoff is requested exactly when configured triggers contain `unknown`, with `triggeredBy:["unknown"]`. The revised text agrees with §§8.1 and 8.3.

- **Examples and alternatives.** The stripped pass-through pack is structurally valid and has valid references. With the stated `goodStanding: true`, absent or numeric `refundAmount` affects no Core condition, so its ordinary Core result is `approve-refund`. Both quantity-reading rules in each cited pack use `onUnknown: escalate`: expense lines 91 and 128; supplier lines 102 and 143. The revised no-change alternative correctly distinguishes that rule-level protection from a requirement attached to the selected outcome.

- **Source attribution and independence.** RFC 0002 supports the reduced composition account without the removed mapping gloss. RFC 0006, lines 33–44 and 237–243, and GOVERNANCE, lines 171–174, support the revised independence caveat. RFC 0000’s stable-feature bar remains unmet; this does not prevent publishing a Draft. RFC 0007 supports the computation boundary and supplies no measured demand for outcome values. RFC 0014 keeps lineage machinery outside Core, and `docs/non-goals.md` supports the stated language boundary.

# v0.6.0

`0.6.0`, tagged `v0.6.0`, is the Core release that carries the dialect formats `4.0.0` and `4.1.0`
and a workflow stage that names them (#326, #328). Packs declare exact `specVersion` `0.6.0`, and
the tag equals the selector, as for `0.5.0`.

- **Core's rules are `0.5.0`'s.** No carrier, structural, semantic or evaluation rule differs. A
  `0.5.0` pack whose selector alone is changed to `0.6.0` keeps every verdict and every disposition
  (Core §11). The selector moves because this release changes what the companion texts admit, and a
  tag `vX.Y.0` carries `specVersion` `X.Y.0` ([VERSIONING.md](../VERSIONING.md)).
- **The selector.** A `0.6.0` reader refuses a pack declaring `0.5.0`, by the exact selector, and a
  `0.5.0` reader refuses one declaring `0.6.0`. Changing the selector changes the pack's bytes and
  digest.
- Tag `v0.5.0` still identifies Core `0.5.0`, and `v0.5.1` and `v0.5.2` carry it unchanged. Its
  schemas stay published at their `0.5.0` identifiers, byte for byte, and its text and corpora stay
  at those tags.

## Candidate period

None. The maintainer waived the candidate period: no release candidate precedes `v0.6.0`, and no
adopter period was run.

## Included work

- **Dialect format `4.0.0` (`spec/dialect.md` §2.2).** A rule on the case, list and decimal
  parameter types, rule version `2.0.0` with ordered comparison, membership, a forward freshness
  window and literal claim values, and three built-in derived values in exact integer arithmetic.
  RFC 0018's amendments of 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13 are adopted for `0.6.0`; the format was first
  carried, as proposed, by `v0.5.1`.
- **Dialect format `4.1.0`, a paged `operation` source (`spec/dialect.md` §2.3, #328).**
  [RFC 0033](../rfcs/0033-paged-operation-source.md) is accepted. An `operation` source may declare
  `paging`: its pages are one gateway session's calls 0 to n−1, each with its own signed receipt,
  each request after the first formed from the first and the page before; the loop ends at a page
  that names no next reference or at a declared bound, and the preparation records which. The
  pages' records are joined in call order, duplicates kept, and the source's read applies once.
  `pageItems` stays refused. A `prevSignature` at odds with its receipt's own call index is
  `acquisition-invalid` (#326).
- **Recordings of both formats (#326).** Version 2 of the dialect-recording schemas,
  `schema/dialect-recording/2/`, informative, describes a preparation of format `4.0.0` or `4.1.0`
  and an export carrying one. Version 1 is unchanged and reads the earlier forms.
- **A workflow stage of either format (#326).** RFC 0020's amendment of 2026-11-15 is accepted for
  `0.6.0`: such a stage takes no Runner encoding, and its release digest is SHA-256 of the `canon`
  bytes of the whole retained release, which is open beyond `id`, `packDigest`, `runtimeDigest` and
  `inputMapping`. The workflow document schema of `0.6.0` adds both formats to a named dialect
  reference. RFC 0020 stays a Draft, and `spec/workflow.md` stays normative for nothing.
- **Readers.** The Python dialect reader reads both formats; the TypeScript reader refuses both
  `version-unread`. The workflow reader reads a stage of either, digests its release by the stage's
  rule, and checks a paged source's profile fit.
- **Tests that do not read the live tree (#319).** The patch-release tests hold the builder to a
  hand-authored fixture and a test-local normative record, so they do not change when `main` moves
  to a new `specVersion`.

The reviews are those recorded on pull requests 329, 330, 331 and 332, by a model of the drafting
vendor. That is same-vendor review, not a cross-vendor claim; the maintainer waived the review by
another vendor for this work.

## Compatibility

- **Reader compatibility:** a `0.5.0` reader refuses a pack declaring `0.6.0`, by the exact
  selector. A dialect reader written to an earlier text refuses a document of format `4.1.0`
  `input-malformed`, and one written to this text that does not read a format refuses it
  `version-unread`; neither reads part of one.
- **Writer compatibility:** a writer targeting `0.5.0` keeps emitting `0.5.0`. A dialect with no
  paged source has no reason to take format `4.1.0`.
- **Semantic compatibility:** for every pack, `0.6.0` changes no result. No answer, digest,
  preparation or vector of dialect format `2`, `3`, `2.0.0`, `3.0.0` or `4.0.0` changes, and no
  release digest of a stage of format `2.0.0` or `3.0.0`, or of integer version `2` or `3`, changes.
- **Migration compatibility:** changing only the selector is enough (Core §11).

## Migration note

Packs targeting this release declare exact `specVersion` `0.6.0`; nothing else in a pack changes.
The edit changes the bytes, so the pack should receive a new `version`. A conformance claim is made
against exact `specVersion` `0.6.0` and the corpora published with it, and is not inherited from
`0.5.0`. Multiple-version readers dispatch on the complete selector and keep the `0.5.0` artifacts
beside the new ones.

## What is in the bundles

The `.tar.gz` and `.zip` bundles, with their checksums and build-provenance attestations, hold:

- the Core and companion prose under `spec/`, without the Draft `spec/workflow.md`;
- `schema/`, now with `schema/dialect-recording/2/`, and `conformance/`, without the Policy
  companion's directories or the staged directories (`conformance/annotation-vocabulary/`,
  `conformance/dialect/`, `conformance/witness/`, `conformance/evaluation/staged/`);
- `examples/`;
- `README.md`, `LICENSE`, `NOTICE`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `VERSIONING.md`, `TESTING.md` and `SECURITY.md`;
  and
- this file.

They do not hold `catalog/`, which a patch release's bundles carry, `rfcs/`, `tools/`,
`requirements-dev.txt`, the other files in `releases/`, the leaf inventories, or a generated
`release-identity.json`: this release has no distribution mapping, because its tag is its
`specVersion`. The vectors of formats `4.0.0` and `4.1.0`, of the recording schemas and of the
workflow stages are under `conformance/dialect/`, so no bundle carries them.

## What this release does not establish

- **A different-vendor review.** The work was accepted on reviews by a model from the drafting
  vendor, and the maintainer waived the review RFC 0009 asks for.
- **A conformance claim for either dialect format.** Their vectors are hand-written, in no corpus
  and no bundle, and no claim names either format (`spec/dialect.md` §14).
- **A second reader of either format.** One of the repository's two dialect readers reads them; the
  TypeScript reader refuses both `version-unread`.
- **The reference Runner's agreement.** The reference Runner prepares paged sources since its pull
  request 299 (`v0.34.0`). This repository runs none of its tests, and no Runner recording of
  either format is among the vectors.
- **Recordings under `0.5.0` or `0.6.0`.** The recording readers read `0.2.0-draft` and `0.3.0`
  recordings alone; reading later ones is a follow-up.
- **The annotation vocabulary under `0.6.0`.** The vocabulary `1.0.0` and its checker apply to
  `specVersion` `0.5.0`; admitting `0.6.0` is a follow-up.

## Checks run for this release

The release reuses `main`'s `repository-conformance` evidence for this exact commit through the
release evidence gate (`scripts/dev/release_evidence.py`, configured by `.github/ci-evidence.json`)
instead of running the repository suite again. The milestone, tag, artifact-pin and bundle checks
run again at the tag. The reused run's id and attempt are in the gate's decision record, which the
release run keeps as its `release-evidence-<attempt>` artifact (`web/DEPLOYMENT.md`, "Release
evidence").

Results against this release name its tag and commit. Conformance never proves truth, authorization,
safety or operational fitness.

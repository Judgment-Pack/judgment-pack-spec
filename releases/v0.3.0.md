# v0.3.0

`0.3.0`, tagged `v0.3.0`, is the first Core release whose `specVersion` has no `-draft` suffix.
The released `0.2.0-draft` identifier and its tag keep their names. Two GitHub pre-releases came
before it, and both remain immutable with their mappings and notes:

- [`v0.3.0-rc.1`](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-spec/releases/tag/v0.3.0-rc.1),
  published 2026-10-25, carried the renamed manifest maturity (`status` `draft` in place of the
  retired `research-preview`) and the re-pin of the Core schemas, manifests, packs, vectors,
  readers and site routes to exact `specVersion` `0.3.0` (#231).
- [`v0.3.0-rc.2`](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-spec/releases/tag/v0.3.0-rc.2),
  published 2026-10-27, added exactly the selected `retained-column/1` leaf, accepted at draft
  maturity through [RFC 0029](../rfcs/0029-retained-column-conversions.md) (#274).

The leaf is part of this release. Packs declare `specVersion` `0.3.0`; neither candidate's
distribution identity is a pack version selector.

## Included work

- Core escalation routes from RFC 0025: an exclusive single target or bounded conditional routes,
  with routed handoff provenance and complete retained reasons.
- The `base` and `extended` document ceiling profiles from RFC 0026, including reader negotiation
  and explicit refusal of an unsupported profile.
- The non-normative rule-overlap check and its closed `JPS-RULES-OVERLAP` diagnostic.
- RFC 0020's bulk amendment: contributing-rule output records, disposition summaries, reproducible
  seeded samples, drift comparison, and the closed workflow schema and hand-written fixture that
  verify those records.
- RFC 0020's later-sibling rule, limited to instances admitted for acquisition so an admission
  failure retains its own finding.
- Dialect data-quality conversions, reconciliation, typed research-result states, lineage,
  versioned source profiles, resolved settings, and byte-retaining replay contracts.
- RFC 0022's alignment with the landed composition decisions for subgraph bounds, routed handoff
  provenance, dialect-owned remapping, and cross-installation packaging.
- The selected `retained-column/1` leaf from RFC 0029: its companion
  [selected retained columns](../spec/retained-column.md), its versioned schema and corpus, and the
  two offline readers, at draft maturity.

## The selected retained-column leaf

The release bundles carry the leaf's schema, corpus, companion prose and both offline readers with
the bytes `v0.3.0-rc.2` published. The final inventory `releases/v0.3.0.retained-column.json`
names distribution `v0.3.0` and records the same schema, corpus and file digests as the
candidate's inventory; the release builder refuses a final whose inventory differs from the
candidate's in anything but that name.

The leaf's support tuple is the one its companion states: contract `retained-column/1`,
distribution version `0.3.0-rc.2`, the candidate's source commit, and the schema and corpus
digests below. It is independent of the pack `specVersion`, and declaring `0.3.0` does not select
the leaf. The final bundle has no generated `release-identity.json`, so its readers answer a
retained-column selection with `capability-unsupported`; a selected-leaf support claim is made
against an installation of the `v0.3.0-rc.2` distribution. The leaf's support claim is limited to
its named scope: it is no general Core, stable interoperability or general preparation-verifier
conformance claim.

## Versioning and maturity

Pre-`1.0.0` Core releases may be incompatible when their notes record the change and give a
`specVersion` migration note. Conformance manifest `status` is now the independent maturity ladder
`draft`, `candidate`, and `release`; `0.3.0` remains `draft`. The `release` maturity is reserved for
the `1.0.0` standards claim after the governance acceptance evidence is met and recorded. Production
use does not by itself establish that claim.

The Policy companion remains on its independent `policy-v1.0.0-draft.N` route until that format's
first release and is not renamed by the Core scheme change. This release leaves it unchanged: the
Core bundles exclude its schemas and corpus, and RFC 0023 stays Accepted at draft maturity with
format `1.0.0` pinned (#211).

RFC 0024 is not part of this release; it remains a Draft.

## Migration note

Packs targeting this release declare exact `specVersion` `0.3.0`. Reader and writer compatibility
is not inferred from a shared `0.x` prefix: historical readers reject this selector, and `0.3.0`
profiles and routed escalations require corresponding reader support. Writers targeting
`0.2.0-draft` must obey its historical schema, which admits neither `profile` nor escalation `routes`.
Multiple-version readers dispatch on the complete selector; they do not apply current rules to old
selectors or rewrite old documents on read.

Carrier admission is tighter: Unicode escapes in the UTF-16 surrogate range must form well-formed
surrogate pairs.
For example, a lone `\uD800` title escape accepted by the published `0.2.0-draft` carrier and
structural schema is malformed in `0.3.0`. A version-only edit cannot repair those bytes. The author
must provide the intended scalar or valid surrogate pair; automatic replacement would lose meaning.

Selector-only migration is sufficient only for documents that already meet the current carrier,
structural and semantic requirements. Revalidate profile/ceiling support and escalation shapes;
otherwise review and edit the content deliberately. No general automatic migration or unchanged
conformance-verdict promise is made. Re-declaration also selects the exact new evaluator semantics
and corpus, not inherited evaluator conformance. The Runtime must vendor the exact resulting
artifacts beside historical releases and retain exact-selector dispatch; it must not overwrite the
existing `0.2.0-draft` artifacts.
